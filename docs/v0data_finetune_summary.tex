\section{Summary}
One AlphaGenome~\cite{avsec2026} model was fine-tuned jointly on five single-cell multiome
studies (ten species-specific sources, 20 output heads). It starts from the
AlphaGenome FOLD\_1 checkpoint and is evaluated on genome regions that this
checkpoint never saw as pretraining targets. The most effective changes were to
the training targets: RNA rebuilt as gene-level pseudobulk counts per million
(CPM) from raw counts, ATAC rebuilt from raw fragments where released tracks
were unsuitable, and removal of near-empty, unsupported or artifactual signal.
On held-out regions, frozen-backbone heads reach mean correlation $R=0.455$,
and adding low-rank adapters raises it to $0.500$--$0.507$ (ATAC $0.66$--$0.67$,
RNA $0.33$--$0.35$). LoRA and LoRA with convolutional adapters (LoCon) perform
similarly. On fine-mapped brain eQTLs, gene-span variant effects give AUROC up
to $0.57$ (SingleBrain) and $0.64$ (XQTL). The frozen-backbone model is best
for eQTLs, adapter training lowers eQTL discrimination, and discrimination is
concentrated within 10 kb of the TSS.

An earlier version of this report fine-tuned from the \texttt{all\_folds}
checkpoint and held out chromosomes 8 and 9, which that checkpoint was
pretrained on. Its held-out results (mean test $R$ $0.627$) are superseded.

\paragraph{Held-out regions.} AlphaGenome's FOLD\_1 checkpoint~\cite{avsec2026} was
pretrained without two of the eight Borzoi genome folds~\cite{linder2025}, one
used here for validation and one for test. Input windows of 131,072 bp are assigned to training, validation
or test by these folds, directly for human and mouse and through liftover of
4 kb bins to hg38 for macaque and marmoset, so that sequence orthologous to
held-out human regions is also held out. Windows that overlap more than one
split are dropped (0.4--4.4\% per source), and validation and test windows are
subsampled to about 1,000 per source.

\section{Training strategies}
Performance is measured by signed double-centered Pearson correlation $R$. For
target and prediction matrices $Y,\hat{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}$, with $N$
genomic elements (genes for RNA, 128 bp bins for ATAC) and $C$ cell groups, row
and column means are removed before computing the correlation, so $R$ measures
relative structure across elements and cell groups rather than assay scale.
The comparisons below come from matched pairs of earlier runs that differed
in one setting; those runs used the chromosome split, so their absolute values
are inflated but the differences between arms are informative.

\paragraph{RNA targets and loss.} Released coordinate RNA tracks were replaced
with gene-level targets. Raw counts are summed over the cells of each group
before CPM normalization; summing per-cell normalized expression instead gives
targets whose double-centered agreement with the correct ones is only
$0.46$--$0.54$ (Johansen). The model's RNA output $\hat{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times
C}$ over $B$ 128 bp bins is read out per gene $g$ as \begin{equation}
\hat{y}_{g,c}=\sum_{b} w_{g,b}\,\hat{x}_{b,c},
\end{equation}
where $w_{g,b}\in[0,1]$ is the fraction of bin $b$ covered by exons of $g$,
and the loss is the squared error between $\log(1+\hat{y}_{g,c})$ and
$\log(1+y_{g,c})$. In a matched frozen-backbone comparison on Zemke 2023 that
changed only this target, test RNA $R$ rose from $0.152$ to $0.421$ with ATAC
unchanged. The gene-level target also avoids a ribosomal-repeat artifact in the
released marmoset RNA tracks. Adding a per-bin coverage loss did not help (HDA,
mean validation $R$ $0.604$ with versus $0.598$ without), so it was dropped.

\paragraph{ATAC targets.} Where released tracks were significance scores or
poorly matched coverage, ATAC was rebuilt from raw fragments as full-depth
fragment coverage per million fragments, without downsampling. For Johansen
human, test $R$ was $0.210$ with released tracks and $0.458$ with the rebuilt
target under the same training setup.

\paragraph{Quality control.} One Johansen group with 33,424 fragments (median
$1.1\times 10^8$) contributed 48\% of all squared ATAC signal and was removed.
Groups without supporting cell assignments are masked rather than given zero
targets: four Zemke 2024 subtype channels and the Zemke 2023 L5 IT RNA group.
The released Zemke 2023 ATAC tracks contain bins with signal of 3,000 to
60,000 RPKM in every cell group, mostly poly-G/C runs, against genome-wide
99.99th percentiles of 97 to 169. In the macaque and marmoset held-out sets a
single such bin or window carried 51--89\% of the double-centered variance, so
validation and test $R$ for these heads measured one or two loci. Following the
idea of the ENCODE blacklist~\cite{amemiya2019}, a blacklist
of bins whose minimum across cell groups exceeds ten times the 99.99th
percentile, and of windows whose summed signal exceeds ten times the median
window, flags 9 to 19 regions per species. Excluding them removes one held-out
window per affected split and leaves no held-out window with more than 4\% of
the variance. The blacklist is applied at evaluation; the training windows
still contain these regions.

\paragraph{Loss.} A double-centered correlation term, $1-R$ computed within
each batch, was added to the loss of every RNA head (weight $1$, HDA $0.1$) and
of the Zemke 2023 ATAC heads (weight $3$). In a matched continuation it raised
validation $R$ from $0.568$ to $0.583$, mainly through RNA ($0.461$ to $0.491$).

\paragraph{Model and optimization.} The pretrained backbone is shared by all
heads. Each source has its own ATAC and RNA heads, because target
distributions differ between species of the same study; with equal sampling of
sources this raised LoRA test $R$ from $0.584$ to $0.605$ relative to one pooled
head per study and modality, a comparison that changes both factors together.
Randomly initialized heads are first trained on a frozen backbone; the best
warmup epoch then starts adapter training. Adapters are LoRA~\cite{hu2021} (rank 16) on
attention and pair projections, optionally with LoCon~\cite{yeh2024} (rank 4)
on downsampling blocks 2--5, at learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$; learning rate $10^{-3}$ was
unstable for LoCon. Initializing heads from pretrained neural accessibility
tracks improved early warmup validation $R$ ($0.507$ against $0.488$ at epoch
4) but was not used in the final runs.

\paragraph{Not helpful.} Higher adapter rank (LoRA 32, LoCon 8), uniform RNA
loss upweighting, target value transforms, refitting heads after adapter
training, and gene-balanced batch ordering did not improve held-out $R$.

\section{Held-out results}
The frozen-backbone warmup selected epoch 5 by validation $R$. Both adapter
arms were trained from it to epoch 18 and selected epoch 18 (LoRA) and epoch 15
(LoRA+LoCon). Table~\ref{tab:heldout} lists test $R$ per source with the Zemke
2023 ATAC blacklist applied. Adapters raise mean test $R$ from $0.455$ to
$0.507$ (LoRA) and $0.500$ (LoRA+LoCon). LoRA+LoCon is slightly better for ATAC
($0.667$ against $0.661$) and worse for RNA ($0.333$ against $0.353$), so the two
adapter strategies are effectively tied. RNA remains the weaker modality and
varies between sources, from about $0.2$ (Johansen marmoset, HDA with LoCon,
Liu) to $0.46$ (Zemke 2023 marmoset). Split-half reliability gives ATAC
correlation ceilings of $0.82$--$0.93$ for Johansen and $0.90$ for Zemke 2024,
and RNA ceilings of $0.93$ or higher where measured, so RNA is far below its
noise limit.

\section{Fine-mapped eQTL benchmark}
Positives are variants with SuSiE~\cite{wang2020} posterior inclusion
probability (PIP) above $0.75$ and negatives have PIP below $0.01$, within
512 kb of the gene's TSS.
SingleBrain has 35 fine-mapping panels in seven brain cell classes (54,193
variants), with summary statistics labeled as mmQTL~\cite{zeng2022} analyses;
XQTL has six cell-type panels (10,452 variants). Each variant is
scored with the human Zemke 2023 RNA head on a 1 Mb window centered at the
TSS. The gene-span effect is the absolute alternate-minus-reference
difference in mean prediction over the gene's bins, averaged over the tracks
matched to the panel's cell type. The TSS-bin summary performs worse and is
not shown.

Table~\ref{tab:eqtl} compares the three models with one selected pretrained
AlphaGenome track per cell type. The frozen-backbone model is best on both
datasets, and adapter training lowers pooled AUROC by $0.01$--$0.06$. The native tracks were
selected on the same variants, so their values are optimistic. Discrimination
is concentrated near the TSS: AUROC is $0.65$--$0.69$ (SingleBrain) and
$0.83$--$0.88$ (XQTL) within 1 kb and falls below $0.5$ for all models beyond
about 50 kb (SingleBrain) and 100 kb (XQTL) (Figure~\ref{fig:distance}).

The trained tracks carry a small amount of cell-type-specific signal. Scoring
each panel with every cell class's tracks, the matched class exceeds the
unmatched ones by $0.012$--$0.028$ AUROC on SingleBrain and $0.039$--$0.044$ on
XQTL, which is significant under permutation of the class assignment for every
model ($p\le 0.006$). The matched composite ranks first for microglia panels in
every model and dataset and for most astrocyte and oligodendrocyte panels,
whereas excitatory and inhibitory neuron panels show little specificity.
Predicted effects are largely shared across cell types, with median Spearman
correlation $0.61$--$0.72$ between tracks.

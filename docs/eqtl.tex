\section{Overview}
We evaluate whether sequence-derived mutation effects distinguish fine-mapped
single-cell expression quantitative trait loci (eQTLs). A positive is a
variant with posterior inclusion probability (PIP) greater than $0.75$ and a
negative is a variant with PIP less than $0.01$. The evaluation uses human
fine-mapping panels and a $1\,\mathrm{Mb}$ sequence window centered on the
transcription start site (TSS) of the associated gene. RNA-seq denotes RNA
sequencing and CAGE denotes Cap Analysis of Gene Expression. Average precision
(AP) is reported as area under the precision-recall curve (AUPR); AUROC denotes
area under the receiver operating characteristic curve.

The primary comparison is a trained head-only model versus the two best
available joint fine-tuned models, a low-rank adaptation (LoRA) model and a
LoRA model with convolutional low-rank adapters (LoCon). The benchmark retains
up to 1,000 variants in each PIP class per fine-mapping panel with a fixed
random seed. The raw and retained counts are recorded with the benchmark
output.

\section{Mutation effects}
For variant $i$, let $\mathbf{y}_{i,r}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C}$ and
$\mathbf{y}_{i,a}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times C}$ be the reference and alternate
RNA predictions at the model's 128 base-pair (bp) resolution, where $B$ is the number of
output bins and $C=19$ is the number of RNA tracks. Let $\mathcal{C}_g$ be the
track indices for the matched cell group. The TSS score is \begin{equation}
s_i^{\mathrm{TSS}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_g|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_g}\left|y_{i,a,b_{\mathrm{TSS}},c}-y_{i,r,b_{\mathrm{TSS}},c}\right|,
\end{equation}
where $b_{\mathrm{TSS}}$ is the 128 bp bin containing the TSS. The gene score
is the corresponding mean absolute alternate-reference difference after
averaging each track over all 128 bp bins overlapping the annotated gene
span, \begin{equation}
s_i^{\mathrm{gene}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{C}_g|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}_g}\left|\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_i|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_i}y_{i,a,b,c}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_i|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal{B}_i}y_{i,r,b,c}\right|,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{B}_i$ is the set of bins overlapping the gene span. Both
scores are nonnegative because fine-mapping identifies variant probability,
not the direction of expression change. Performance is measured by AUROC
and average precision, overall and within bins of $|\mathrm{variant}-\mathrm{TSS}|$.

\subsection{Native track specificity}
The native AlphaGenome evaluation stores signed alternate-minus-reference
effects for 54,729 sampled variants and 1,213 valid pretrained channels, 667
RNA-seq and 546 CAGE. Predictions are reduced on device to a 128 bp TSS bin
and the mean over the annotated gene span. Of the variants, 54,193 have finite
effects and 536 were skipped because the reference allele did not match the
reference genome. Absolute effects are scored separately for each biosample
against the positive and negative variants in each panel group; strand and
channel tracks are grouped by source, biosample, and assay. Ranks are among the
371 RNA-seq or 273 CAGE biosample groups. The full rank distribution is shown
in Figure~\ref{fig:native_track_ranks}.

Monocyte tracks are the clearest microglia proxy. On MG panels, generic
monocyte CAGE ranks first of 273 biosample groups for TSS AP ($0.320$) and
second for gene-span AP ($0.308$), versus unmatched-track medians of $0.261$
and $0.242$. The CD14-positive, CD16-negative classical monocyte CAGE track
ranks seventh for TSS AP ($0.306$) and fourth for gene-span AP ($0.295$).
CD14-positive monocyte RNA-seq ranks 27th of 371 for TSS AP ($0.307$) and 47th
for gene-span AP ($0.332$). This supports CD14-positive monocytes as a useful
prediction proxy for these eQTL panels, without equating monocytes with
microglia biologically.

There is no mature oligodendrocyte-labeled native RNA-seq or CAGE channel. The
single oligodendrocyte-precursor CAGE channel ranks 226th of 273 on OPC TSS AP
($0.354$) and 246th on gene-span AP ($0.336$), below the unmatched-track
medians ($0.362$ and $0.345$). Bulk brain tracks perform better: dorsolateral
prefrontal cortex RNA-seq ranks first of 371 for OD panels on both summaries
(AP $0.567$ and $0.607$), and brain-region tracks also rank highly for OPC
panels. For OPC panels, dorsolateral prefrontal cortex RNA-seq ranks first for
TSS AP and third for gene-span AP. These are broad neural-tissue proxies, not evidence of
oligodendrocyte-specific prediction. Astrocyte tracks are weakly ranked;
endothelial tracks are moderately enriched over unmatched tracks. The available
glutamatergic and generic neuronal tracks provide mixed results, and there is
no inhibitory-neuron-specific native channel.

These track ranks are descriptive rather than a formal statistical test of

differ across cell classes, and most fine-mapping labels have no exact native
counterpart.

\section{Implementation details}
Fine-mapping tables are streamed from compressed PIP files. Variant alleles
are checked against the reference genome before constructing the alternate
one-hot sequence. The same reference and alternate sequences are evaluated
for every model. The head-only checkpoint was trained on the target tracks
without LoRA or LoCon adapters; the fine-tuned models retain trained heads and
adapters. All model prediction calls use the custom forward path so the
fine-tuned heads, rather than the delegated pretrained variant scorer, are
evaluated. Native AlphaGenome variant effect predictions (VEPs) use the original model's valid RNA-seq and
CAGE tracks, with predictions reduced on device to the TSS bin and gene span
before signed alternate-minus-reference effects are stored. The full matrices
and channel/variant metadata accompany the analysis outputs. Candidate tracks
were ranked against all biosample groups separately for each panel and summary;
the plotted annotations distinguish direct labels, neuronal/monocyte proxies,
oligodendrocyte-precursor tracks, and bulk brain tissue.
